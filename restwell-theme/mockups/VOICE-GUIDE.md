# Restwell voice guide

Reference for keeping copy consistent across mockups, leaflets, welcome-guide updates, and social captions. Derived from analysis of the guest welcome guide ("Welcome to Restwell Retreats," 16 pages), the clearest expression of the voice.

**In one line:** a competent, unhurried friend who happens to run an accessible holiday let — reassuring without being saccharine, precise without being clinical, and gently funny without ever undercutting the practical job the copy has to do.

Five load-bearing habits, if you strip away everything else:
1. Direct address ("you"/"we") in nearly every sentence.
2. A rule or instruction is never given without a reason or a softener attached.
3. Precision (numbers, mm, times, £, addresses) is never sacrificed for warmth — the two coexist in the same sentence.
4. Dry, self-deprecating humour appears roughly once per page, always attached *after* the necessary information, never replacing it.
5. Nearly every section closes with an invitation to make contact — "just ask," "let us know," "give us a ring."

## 1. Sentence-level mechanics

- **Length/rhythm:** short-to-medium (roughly 8–22 words), almost entirely active voice. Compound sentences are common but rarely stack more than two clauses.
- **Contractions:** used constantly (*we're, you'll, it's, don't, can't*) — this is core to the informal register. Full forms ("we are," "it is") are reserved for formal or safety-adjacent moments. **The register visibly tightens when the stakes are higher** (emergency numbers, key contacts avoid contractions almost entirely).
- **Sentence openers**, a small repeatable toolkit:
  - *We-openers* state intent/care: "We love dogs!"
  - *If-openers* handle exceptions gracefully: "If you need to arrive earlier... please contact us in advance."
  - *Please-openers* soften instructions: "Please only flush toilet paper."
  - *This-openers* frame a section's purpose: "This page explains..."
- **Exclamation marks:** sparing, deliberate, attached to humour or the warmest reassurance beats — never to hard facts or numbers.
- **Em/en dashes:** rare in body copy, reserved for gentle asides.
- **Ellipses:** a heading-only device for a wistful, unhurried trail-off ("Before You Head Home…"). Never in body copy.
- **Bold:** functional, not decorative — marks either the single most operationally important phrase in a sentence, or a scannable sub-heading. Never used purely for emphasis on adjectives/feelings.

## 2. Two-register system

- **Register A — Conversational/relational:** "We've worked with the Continuity of Care Services team to create a retreat where you can simply be." Used for anything emotional, reassuring, or scene-setting.
- **Register B — Neutral/factual:** "Sainsbury's — Reeves Way, Chestfield, CT5 3QS — Mon–Sat: 07:00–22:00." Used for tables, contacts, addresses, hours, prices. The voice steps back and lets facts speak — never make emergency numbers or prices cute.

The skill is switching between them within the same page without it feeling jarring: a warm framing paragraph immediately above a stripped-back data table.

## 3. The "rule wrapped in reassurance" pattern (the single most repeated device)

**[The rule] → [why it exists / what it protects] → [an open door if it doesn't work for you]**

| Rule | Reason given | Open door |
|---|---|---|
| Max 5 guests | "It keeps the bungalow comfortable, and it's what our safety checks are based on." | *(none — genuine safety limit)* |
| Check-out by 11am | *(implicit — next guest turnover)* | "If you need extra time for accessibility reasons... let us know a few days in advance and we'll do our best to help." |
| Only flush toilet paper | "Our plumbing is 'retro'... and can block easily!" | Bathroom bin provided as the alternative |
| Report damages | "Accidents happen, and honestly, we'd always rather know!" | Framed as help, not confession |

**Not every rule gets an exception.** Genuine hard limits (guest cap, plumbing) get a reason but no negotiation; soft limits (check-in/out times) get both a reason and a workaround. The voice is warm, but it isn't infinitely flexible — and it's honest about which is which. Don't invent a workaround for a hard limit just to sound nicer.

## 4. Humour: mechanics and placement rules

Roughly once per page. Easy to overdo or misplace — treat these as hard rules, not vibes:

1. **Always after the necessary fact, never instead of it or before it.** Bins get emptied and explained first; the joke about their "unsupervised Whitstable wander" comes last, as a reward for reading.
2. **The joke's target is always an inanimate object, an animal, or the building — never the guest.** ("We've asked the seagulls to do the same, but they're yet to sign the agreement!")
3. **Dry and English, not zany.** No wordplay-heavy puns, no exclamation-stacked enthusiasm, no internet-speak. A warm, slightly wry aunt, not a startup's Twitter account.
4. **Low, evenly spread frequency.** Never two jokes back to back. Never on a safety-critical page (Key Contacts, emergencies, Using the Bathroom hard rules carry zero jokes).

## 5. Accessibility language — the part most likely to go wrong elsewhere

- **Never a separate, bolted-on section.** Threaded through every page as a baseline expectation, not a special add-on.
- **Precision replaces euphemism.** Door widths in mm (965mm/926mm), scooter weight limits in stone, never "extra wide" or "suitable for most users." Vague accessibility claims read as a red flag to disabled guests who've been burned before — this copy visibly avoids that trap.
- **Equipment is guest-owned during the stay, not host-owned and merely lent.** "The bungalow's accessibility kit is here to be used!" — "used," not "available," signals permission rather than tolerance.
- **Tone lifts to genuinely aspirational language specifically around accessibility** — this is the brand's actual value proposition, not a compliance box: "We believe accessibility shouldn't come at the expense of comfort."
- **Third-party accessibility info gets the same precision, including caveats where accuracy can't be guaranteed** ("Accessibility varies from place to place... we always recommend calling ahead"). Honest, not reassuring-for-its-own-sake — never oversell a venue the brand can't vouch for.
- **Guest testimonials are verbatim.** Do not rewrite, shorten, or sanitise attributed reviews (including if a guest used “fully accessible”). The banned phrase still applies to Restwell-authored titles, body, meta, FAQs, alt, and schema. Keep quotes inside `<blockquote>` with a cite so they read as the guest’s words, not the brand’s claim.

**Template:** [aspirational/values framing] → [precise, numeric, verifiable detail] → [invitation to ask for what's missing]
> "We can't promise every extra piece of equipment on short notice, but we'd rather hear from you early than have you worry. If there's a specific adaptation... email hello@restwellretreats.co.uk and we'll do what we can."

## 6. Structural templates by content type

- **Page opener:** [what this page is] + [why you're being given it] + [permission for how to use it]. "This guide covers the essentials of everyday living in the bungalow... Our goal is to make the space feel like yours."
- **Sub-heading + short paragraph** (mini-FAQ rhythm): bold micro-heading (2–4 words, often a question) → 1–3 sentence answer. Also structurally close to what AEO/FAQ blocks need.
- **Venue/place listing:** [Name] → [1–2 sentence sensory/narrative description] → **Accessibility:** [factual line, own bolded label, never buried in prose] → [address] → [phone/web]. Sell and spec are kept separate.
- **Contact/data table:** drops conversational voice almost entirely (Register B) — name, address, hours in a scannable table. Voice re-enters only in the framing sentence above/below, never inside the table.
- **Departure/checklist:** short intro reassurance → checklist of terse imperative action items, no humour, no softening (the voice trusts the relationship enough by this point to just instruct) → CTA → sign-off contact block.

## 7. Lexicon

**Cosy/homely (use liberally):** sanctuary · settle in · at your own pace · pop the kettle on · rest · relax · comfort · effortless · cosier · at home · simply be · linger · savour · breath

**Precision/trust (use liberally, never softened):** step-free · level-access · wheelchair accessible · mm measurements · weight limits · CQC-rated · disabled parking · accessible toilet · lift access · grab rails

**Avoid on sight:**
- Boutique-hospitality vocabulary: "elevate," "curated," "bespoke," "unwind," "indulge," "luxury experience," "unparalleled," "exquisite," "seamless," "world-class," "breathtaking," "stunning," "unforgettable," "nestled," "idyllic."
- Corporate hedge-speak: "we endeavour to," "in the unlikely event that," "please be advised."
- Infantilising accessibility language: "special needs," "differently abled," "inspirational." Disability is discussed practically, not sentimentally.
- Vague superlatives. Claims are specific and falsifiable ("18-metre catamaran") rather than vague ("amazing," "incredible"). Hedges like "around 15 minutes" are fine for walk/drive times only — never for hard specs like doorway width or price.

**Signature recurring phrases** (usable as brand catchphrases): "Rest Easy, Stay Well." · "...and we'll do our best to help." · "Just let us know" / "just ask" · "we'd rather hear from you early than have you worry" · "It's time to holiday at your own pace."

## 8. Formatting conventions

- **Bold** = operational importance or scannable sub-heading, never decorative emphasis on adjectives/feelings.
- **Italics** = taglines, table section labels, literary/elevated page titles — signals "brand voice/flourish" vs. roman "information."
- **Headers** can be evocative/incomplete phrases; **body copy stays plain-spoken**. Keep that contrast rather than making headers as brochure-y as the marketing-page norm.
- **Numbers/data** (phone, postcode, times, prices, mm) always given in full and exact — never rounded or vague, except walk/drive times ("around 15 minutes").
- **QR codes / scan-to-X:** always paired with a one-line plain-English explanation of what happens next.

## 9. Tone calibration matrix — warmth scales with stakes

| Content type | Warmth | Humour? | Contractions? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand/values framing | Highest | Yes, light | Yes |
| Comfort/daily living | High | Yes | Yes |
| House rules / soft constraints | Medium-high | Yes | Yes |
| Hard constraints (plumbing, guest cap) | Medium | Minimal | Yes |
| Logistics (check-in/out, travel, pricing) | Medium | No | Yes |
| Local recommendations | Medium-high | No (narrative colour only) | Yes |
| Safety/emergency data | Lowest (neutral) | None | Minimal |
| Departure/CTA | Medium | No | Yes |

**Rule of thumb: the further content sits from physical/legal safety, the more personality it's allowed to carry.** A pricing table or terms page should stay plain and precise (Register B) — don't force jokes or exclamation marks onto it. A "why guests choose us" or values section can carry full warmth.

## 10. Quick-reference checklist before publishing new copy

- [ ] At least 80% of copy in direct "you/we" address?
- [ ] Does every rule/instruction carry a reason or a softener (or both)?
- [ ] Accessibility specs in exact, verifiable units (mm, stone, times), never vague adjectives?
- [ ] Room for one small, dry joke, landing *after* the fact, targeting an object/animal, not the guest — and not forced if there isn't a natural place for one?
- [ ] Section closes with an open invitation to get in touch, where relevant?
- [ ] Avoided boutique-hospitality clichés?
- [ ] Register tightens (fewer contractions, no jokes, no exclamation marks) on anything safety- or emergency-adjacent?
- [ ] Bold/italics used functionally, not decoratively?
- [ ] A genuinely inflexible rule (plumbing, guest cap) stated honestly, not dressed up as negotiable?

## 11. Worked example

❌ *Generic/off-voice:* "Restwell Retreats is a pet-friendly property. Please contact us for more information regarding pet policies."

✅ *On-voice:* "We love dogs! Just let us know in advance so we can check everything's suitable — assistance dogs are always welcome too."

What changed: direct address, exclamation mark at the warmth peak, "just let us know" open-door phrase, "suitable" doing quiet risk-assessment work without sounding bureaucratic, assistance dogs folded in naturally rather than as a separate compliance clause.
