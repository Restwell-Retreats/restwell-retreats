Your situation

Who Restwell is built for

Families, carers, OTs and commissioners use the same published door widths and kit list — then decide if Russell Drive fits before they travel.

  • Guests and families

    Hoist and wet room already fitted; measurements published; a private home, not a hotel room.

  • Carers and support workers

    Separate sleeping and space to assist without blocking hall routes. Ask your council about a Carer’s Assessment under the Care Act 2014 if you need funding for a break.

  • Occupational therapists

    Published doorway widths, hoist and wet-room specs. Ask for unpublished clearances — we’ll measure.

  • Commissioners & social care

    Care Act short breaks; documentation for direct payments, PHB or CHC. Same rates regardless of who we invoice.

Suitability

Planning & respite FAQ

Complex-care planning order, and when a private bungalow beats care-home respite — or doesn’t.

Work in this order: match published access specs to the person’s equipment; set staffing (daytime, sleep-in vs waking night, backup); decide who pays lodging vs care; get kit confirmation in writing; sort medication, transport and emergency contacts. Only lock dates once those are clear.

Start on the Accessibility page for Restwell’s door widths and hoist. Then coordinate carers or Continuity, confirm slings, sort prescriptions, build buffer into travel, and agree a backup if a carer is ill. Enquire with dates and care needs once that list is drafted.

Confirm access details in writing; pack slings, meds, chargers and comfort items the house won’t supply; share care plans with anyone covering the stay; note nearest accessible parking, toilets and pharmacy; keep Restwell and care-provider numbers to hand.

Five lists: access needs; medical; care rota and backup; documents (funding letters, insurance, access statement); comfort and daily living. Tick each against Restwell’s published kit so you don’t pack what is already on site — or assume kit that isn’t.
