## Bedrooms

H2: The room with the hoist, and room for everyone else

The accessible bedroom has a ceiling track hoist running over a profiling bed with a pressure-relieving mattress, a mobile hoist as well, and a standing aid. We set the room up around what you tell us when you book, so that transfers aren’t a scramble at the end of a long journey.

If you need a second profiling bed, say so and we’ll put one in. The layout is arranged per guest rather than fixed. The second bedroom sleeps whoever else is with you, and there’s a double sofa bed in the conservatory. Five is the limit, which keeps the house comfortable and is what our safety checks are based on.

If you use your own slings, do bring them.

## Wet room

H2: A shower you can take your time in

The bathroom is a level-access wet room with a tilt-in-space shower commode chair, a separate height-adjustable stool, and a support rail by the toilet. There’s no lip to negotiate at the entrance.

The basin winds up and down so it can be set to your height rather than the plumber’s, the shower is thermostatic with a capped maximum temperature, and the toilet is a Geberit washlet with a warm-water wash and dryer. There’s a pull-cord alarm in there too.

Models, measurements and safe working loads all live on the access statement, so this page can stay a walk round the house rather than a spec sheet.

## Living and kitchen

The living space opens straight into the kitchen, which is fully equipped and has worktops you can get to, so making a cup of tea doesn’t involve asking anybody to fill the kettle for you. There’s free high-speed WiFi throughout, which matters more than people admit when someone in the party needs to keep an eye on work or a hospital app.

The sofa bed is in the conservatory. It’s a sofa bed in a conservatory, not a third bedroom wearing a disguise.

Pop the kettle on whenever you’re ready.

## Outside

French doors open onto a level patio, the garden and the BBQ area, with a non-slip threshold ramp taking the raised lip off the doorway. Going outside should be a decision, not an expedition.

The driveway is private, level, and takes two cars including an adapted vehicle. We also keep a set of portable fold-up ramps in the house that you’re very welcome to borrow. Take them to a café or a shop and just bring them back before you head home.
