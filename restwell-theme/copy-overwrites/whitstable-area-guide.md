## Intro (`wg_intro`)

Every seaside town has a page like this and most of them are useless, because they list attractions without ever mentioning what’s under your wheels.

So this one is organised around surfaces and loos. Some mornings you’ll want the harbour and something hot to eat on the sea wall. Some days you’ll want Tankerton and a long, level look at the water. Some days you won’t want to leave the garden, and that’s a perfectly good day too.

Do ring ahead for anywhere that matters to you. Old buildings change their arrangements, and we’d rather you knew before you set off than after you’ve parked.

## The harbour and the shingle

Whitstable’s trademark is the shingle beach, the pastel huts and a working harbour where the boats still come in. The beach itself is not a wheelchair route, and we’re not going to pretend a determined push makes it one.

The sea wall and the quays are flat, and that’s where things actually happen. **Whitstable Harbour Market** on the South Quay has artisan stalls and small kitchens doing everything from fried fish to Thai. It’s open weekends year-round with extra days in the school holidays, and you can check dates on 01227 282666. **The Forge** on the sea wall is a seafood shack where you order at a counter and eat outside on flat ground; there’s no loo on site, but the harbour’s public one is just east of it.

It gets genuinely busy in the school holidays. If crowds wear you out, come on a weekday.

## Tankerton promenade

If you want the sea without the shingle, Tankerton is the answer. The promenade is a long level stretch along the clifftop with the water on one side. The grassy slopes running down to the beach are a different proposition altogether.

**JoJo’s** sits along that run: a family-run place doing Mediterranean meze and fresh fish, with wheelchair access and an accessible toilet. It’s about twenty minutes on foot from the bungalow: ten to reach the seafront, then another ten heading west along the promenade itself. That second half is the flat, pleasant bit, and it’s a very short drive if you’d rather save your energy for lunch. They get queues out of the door, so book if you possibly can.

**The Marine Hotel** is on Marine Parade, a Shepherd Neame restaurant below a seaside hotel with estuary views. The lounge and restaurant are on the ground floor with step-free access, and there’s an accessible toilet by reception.

## Round the corner from the house

**The Plough Inn** in Swalecliffe is the closest, a 1930s roadhouse with free parking and a Sunday roast worth going back for. There’s a footpath at the end of the road that brings you out with the pub on your right. We’ll point it out in your welcome pack.

One honest caveat: the building has step-free access but no wheelchair-accessible toilet. We’d rather tell you that here than have you find out mid-roast. Ring them on 01227 794636 if anything has changed.

## Indoors, when the weather turns

**The Horsebridge Arts Centre** on Horsebridge Road is the most reliably accessible building in town: lift access to all three floors, every gallery space wheelchair-accessible, an accessible toilet on the first floor, and a community café downstairs. It’s closed on Tuesdays.

**Whitstable Castle and Gardens** on Tower Hill is free to get into, dogs are welcome, and there are accessible toilets in the main building. The ornamental gardens are the sort of place where half a day disappears and feels like half an hour.

**Whitstable Boat Trips** run year-round on The Venturer, an 18-metre catamaran: a short bay cruise, an hour out to the Maunsell Sea Forts, or seal-spotting in The Swale. It is wheelchair accessible, but do ring 07383 441811 beforehand so they can sort the transfer and secure your chair properly on board.

## Parking

The driveway at the bungalow is the easy part: private, level, room for two cars including an adapted vehicle.

Blue Badge parking in town needs more of a plan, particularly around the harbour, where the ANPR will catch you out if you don’t know that day’s rules. Read the signs properly before you walk away, or ask us and we’ll tell you what we know.

## Getting around without the car

Whitstable station runs direct trains to London, about an hour and a half, and along the Kent coast. There’s a taxi office right by the station exit. We don’t make claims about platform access, because it varies by journey; check National Rail for the specific train you’re getting.

For accessible taxis, **Abacus Cars** on 01227 277745 are the local firm we’d recommend. Book wheelchair-accessible vehicles ahead, especially around school-run hours, when they disappear.

Local buses run frequently through town and along the coast, with the 400 stopping at The Plough. It’s a cheap way to get about and it saves the parking problem entirely.

## Further afield

**Wildwood Trust** near Herne Bay, about half an hour away, is 40 acres of ancient woodland with red squirrels, wolves and beavers. Most paths are usable by wheelchairs and mobility scooters, and they lend both. Book on 01227 209621 because the scooters go quickly. Scooters are £10 per use with a 21-stone limit, and disabled parking on site is free. Being real woodland, there are exposed roots and it gets muddy after rain. The rope bridge over the bear enclosure is the one part nobody can get to.

**Dreamland** in Margate is wheelchair accessible with accessible toilets throughout, though they don’t hire out wheelchairs. For an Essential Companion wristband, apply through Nimbus Disability at least seven days before you go; an existing Nimbus Access Card with a +1 or +2 works on the day at the Main Token Booth. Accessible parking at Arlington Car Park is around 400m from the entrance at £10 a day.

**Canterbury** is twenty minutes away and makes a full day. The Cathedral’s Welcome Centre has free wheelchairs and most of the Cathedral is step-free; disabled parking can be arranged in advance on 01227 762862. For the city itself, Shopmobility on Gravel Walk hire scooters and wheelchairs, Wednesday to Friday 10am–4pm and Saturday 9.30am–4pm, on 01227 459889. Parts of the centre and the Cathedral approach are cobbled. The riverside paths and Westgate Gardens are much smoother going.

## Borrow the ramps

There’s a set of portable fold-up ramps at the bungalow, and they’re yours while you’re staying. Take them with you: a café step, a shop threshold, the one lip between you and a decent lunch. Just bring them home before you check out.

## How far is the sea?

About ten minutes on foot from the driveway. Tankerton promenade is the level stretch once you get there, and everything along it (JoJo’s, the Marine Hotel) adds walking time on top, because you’re then heading west along the prom rather than towards it.

Pace varies and so does the wind coming off the estuary, so treat both numbers as a gentle guide rather than a schedule. If you’d like to know about the surface on a specific route before you commit to it, ask us and we’ll walk it and tell you.
