```text
You are researching how people in the UK actually try to find a holiday that will work when someone is disabled, uses a wheelchair or hoist, needs overnight support, or is an unpaid carer who also needs a break.

You are not writing an SEO strategy for a brand. You are not mapping URLs. You are not recommending titles or keywords for a website.

Put yourself in each person’s shoes. Reconstruct what they are afraid of, what they have already tried, what they type when they do not yet know the “correct” industry words, and what they type after they have been let down by a listing that said accessible and was not.

PEOPLE (research each as a separate journey; do not collapse them into “the accessible travel market”)

1. A disabled adult arranging their own stay. They want a holiday, not a placement. They may be angry at being treated as a patient.
2. A family member organising for someone they live with (partner, parent, adult child). They are tired, they have been burned by vague listings, they will phone if the page does not answer the scary questions.
3. An unpaid carer who also needs rest. They may search “respite” even when they want a house, not a care home. They may feel guilty for wanting a break.
4. An occupational therapist, case manager, or solicitor’s deputy who has to defend the choice in a file. They need numbers and named kit, but that is not how they always start.
5. A commissioner, social worker, or CHC coordinator checking whether a private house plus optional care is even allowed.

For each person, write:
- The situation in the week they start searching (where they are, what just happened).
- The first three things they might type — amateur, messy, possibly “wrong”. Quote autocomplete, People Also Ask, related searches, and forum thread titles. google.co.uk. Date the research.
- What they open, what they distrust, what makes them close the tab.
- How their language changes after they learn the kit words (hoist, profiling bed, wet room, direct payment, CHC) — only if you actually see that shift in forums or SERPs. Do not assume they start with brand names.
- What Google, AI Overviews, ChatGPT-with-search, and Perplexity currently tell them. Quote. Who gets cited. What is missing or misleading.
- What would make them feel safe enough to enquire — in their words, not a brand’s.

METHOD

- Live UK evidence only. Cite URLs. If you cannot see volume, say so. Do not invent volume.
- Prefer forums, Reddit, Mumsnet, Carers UK threads, Euan’s Guide reviews, GOV.UK/NHS/CQC wording, and verbatim PAA over holiday-park marketing copy.
- Treat directory filters (ceiling track hoist, profiling bed) as operator information architecture, not proof of what people type first.
- Do not start from a town or county. If place appears in autocomplete or PAA without you seeding it, record it.
- Do not use Restwell’s millimetres, brand names, or page list as seeds. If Restwell appears in a SERP or AI answer, note it as a finding.
- Be critical: if a common SEO story (“everyone searches ceiling track hoist”) is only supported by a directory URL, say the evidence is weak.
- Separate: (a) words people type, (b) words charities and councils use, (c) words cottage listings use. They are not the same.

OUTPUT

A journey document, not a marketing plan.
```
