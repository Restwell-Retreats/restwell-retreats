## What the data says

**They will not open page 2.** [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/google-ctr-stats) (Semrush GSC, ~4 million results, updated April 2025): **0.63%** of searchers click anything on page 2. Position 1 average CTR **27.6%**. Top 3 take **54.4%** of clicks. Positions 8–10 are almost flat — moving from 10 to 9 barely matters; moving from 2 to 1 is a **74.5%** relative CTR lift.

[Chitika Insights](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/the-value-of-google-result-positioning/65176/) (May 2013, tens of millions of Google-referred ad impressions): page 1 already took **92%** of traffic; moving from page 1 to page 2 dropped traffic **95%**. Old sample, same cliff.

[First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/reports/google-click-through-rates-ctrs-by-ranking-position/) (2026 table, updated May 2025) is a *clean* SERP (no maps, ads, shopping). Position 1 **39.8%**, position 2 **18.7%**, position 10 **1.6%**. Paid position 1 is **2.1%**. Their number is a ceiling; Backlinko is the mixed everyday figure. Use both as a range (roughly **20–40%** for #1), not a forecast.

**The UK is the worst country in SparkToro’s 2026 set for clicks.** [SparkToro + Similarweb](https://sparktoro.com/blog/zero-click-searches-highest-in-the-uk-lowest-in-germany-and-france-has-the-most-efficient-searchers/) (browser panel, Jan–Apr 2026):

| | UK |
| --- | ---: |
| Zero-click searches | 69.5% |
| At least one click | 30.5% |
| Performs another search | 26.4% |
| Session ends on the SERP | 43.1% |
| Open-web clicks per 1,000 searches | 232 |

US zero-click in the sibling study: 68.01% ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/)). Panels changed over the years (Jumpshot → Datos → Similarweb); treat the decade trend as directional, the 2026 UK table as the working number.

**They reformulate instead of paginating.** A Restwell guest who typed `disabled holidays uk`, saw Limitless / Parkdean / directories, and felt nothing fit does not go to page 2. A quarter of UK searches are followed by another query. That second query is cottage, wet room, hoist, “with care”. That is the page 1 Restwell can actually be on.

**AI Overviews steal the remaining clicks, especially on care/health-adjacent jobs.** [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) (900 US adults, 68,879 searches, March 2025): traditional result click **8%** of visits with an AI summary vs **15%** without; **1%** click a link inside the summary; session-end **26%** vs **16%**. [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/) (Dec 2025 vs Dec 2023, 300k keywords): AI Overviews correlate with **−58%** position-1 CTR versus a no-overview forecast. [Sistrix / Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/ai-overviews-publisher-traffic/) (UK, Jan–Apr 2026): first-result CTR **27% → 11%** when an AI Overview sits on top (~60% drop); health is among the most exposed verticals.

**Titles are scanned, then often rewritten.** [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/): F-pattern scan — first line of the title, shorter second pass, down the left. Backlinko: titles **40–60 characters** had the highest CTR (**+8.9%** vs outside that band); keyword-similar URLs **+45%**; positive sentiment **+4.1%**. [Zyppy](https://zyppy.com/seo/google-title-rewrite-study/) (80,959 titles, Q1 2022): Google rewrote **61.6%**. Later industry samples report higher. If title and H1 fight, guests see Google’s line, not ours.

SparkToro’s own caveat still applies to Restwell: branded, local, and high-intent transactional queries are the searches that still send clicks. A wheelchair bungalow with optional CQC care is high-intent. `disabled holidays uk` is not our transactional query.

## What this does to Restwell

1. **Do not spend the site trying to rank page 2 of directory SERPs.** `disabled holidays uk`, `assisted holidays UK`, `holidays with carers provided` are Limitless / Enable / Helping Hands / parks. We will not be clicked there even if we appear. Home stays `accessible holiday cottages by the sea`. Care writes toward adding home care to a self-catering stay, not toward “carers provided”.

2. **Page 1 of the refined query is the whole SEO job.** Wet room, bungalow, cottage by the sea, optional care during a private stay. Those are the searches after the first SERP failed them.

3. **The snippet has to beat nine identical directories in one F-scan.** Query language in the first 40 characters. Differentiator in the rest: one private bungalow, wet room, hoist, add home care via Continuity. Not “accessible holiday cottages UK” again. Not “fully accessible”. Not “or bring your own carer” as the hook (own carer is a given; the USP is adding care).

4. **Meta is risk-reduction, not a slogan.** This purchase is kit + sometimes care. Millimetres and who provides care earn the click. “Learn more” does not.

5. **AI Overviews on care queries are a second SERP.** On 28 Aug 2026, `accessible holiday cottage with care` showed an Overview headed “Arranging Care During Your Stay”. We want that box to describe Restwell’s model (private house, optional domiciliary care, not a centre) and to cite us. Being in the Overview matters even when they do not click.

6. **Match title and H1.** Voice belongs on the page. The title still has to look like the query *and* like a specific house, in 40–60 characters, or Google will replace it with a directory-shaped rewrite.

## Worked snippet (Home)

Overwrite already points this way. Keep it.

- Title: `Accessible holiday cottages by the sea, one bungalow` (52 characters). Query first. “One bungalow” is the click reason against Sykes / National Trust / Accessible Holiday Escapes.
