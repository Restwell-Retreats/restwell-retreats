**[H1]**

## An accessible seaside holiday in Whitstable

**[Intro paragraph]**

Whitstable is oysters and sea air and mornings where the biggest decision is whether to walk to the harbour or the beach first. Restwell Retreats is a holiday bungalow on the Kent coast, five minutes from the seafront, in a town that does seaside properly. It also happens to be fully adapted for wheelchair users and guests with complex mobility needs — ceiling track hoist, profiling bed, roll-in shower, wide doorways throughout. Not as an afterthought. Not as a compromise. Just a good holiday, in a place that actually works.

---

**[H2 — Block 1]**

### A holiday first, accessible as standard

Most accessible accommodation asks you to choose between a holiday you want and a property you can physically use. Restwell doesn't. This is a comfortable, well-equipped bungalow in a residential street in Whitstable — close to the sea, close to the town, close to everything that makes this part of Kent worth visiting. It's not a clinical environment. There's no institutional feel. You won't find laminated safety posters on the walls.

What you will find is a property that's been thoughtfully adapted so that the accessibility features are just part of the fabric. The ceilings have tracking for a hoist because some people need one. The shower is a roll-in wet room because that's what works. The doorways are wide because they should be. Everything you need is here so you can get on with the actual point of being here — having a break.

---

**[H2 — Block 2]**

### What's here for you

The bungalow is fully wheelchair accessible throughout, with level threshold access from the parking area right through to every room. The front door is 965mm wide. Internal doors are 926mm. There's a ceiling track hoist, a profiling bed, a roll-in shower with grab rails, and space for your own equipment if you're bringing it.

The kitchen is set up for self-catering — because eating out in Whitstable is brilliant, but sometimes you just want toast and tea without leaving the house. There's parking right outside for adapted vehicles, and the whole property is single-storey with no steps anywhere.

If you're travelling with a carer or support worker, there's room for them too. And if you're bringing a dog — assistance or otherwise — they're welcome with a bit of advance notice.

We know that accessibility isn't one-size-fits-all, so if you have specific requirements beyond what's already in place, get in touch before you book. We'll be honest about what we can and can't do, and we'd rather have that conversation upfront than have you arrive to find something doesn't work.

---

**[H2 — Block 3]**

### Whitstable and the Kent coast

Whitstable is a proper seaside town — not a resort, not a theme park. It's famous for its oysters, its independent shops, and its harbour where fishing boats still come and go. The high street has more personality per square metre than most towns manage across a whole postcode. There are galleries, cafés, pubs, and restaurants that take their food seriously without taking themselves too seriously.

The seafront stretches from Seasalter in the west through Tankerton and its famous slopes to Swalecliffe in the east — miles of coast with views across to the Isle of Sheppey and, on a clear day, the Essex shore. Canterbury is eight miles away. Faversham is seven. Herne Bay is four. London is an hour and a half by train.

It's a place that rewards slow days. A walk along the front. A pint at the local. Oysters if you want them, fish and chips if you don't. That's the kind of holiday this is.

---

**[H2 — Block 4]**

### Who stays with us

Restwell is for anyone who needs accessible accommodation and wants a genuine holiday — not a medical placement and not a last resort.

We welcome families, couples, solo travellers, and groups. We work with occupational therapists and case managers who are recommending holiday options for their clients. We accept bookings from local authority commissioners and guests using direct payments or personal budgets to fund their stay. And we understand that for many of our guests, finding somewhere that's both properly accessible and actually enjoyable is not easy.

That's what we're here to change.

**[H1]**

## Whitstable and the Kent coast

**[Intro paragraph]**

Restwell Retreats sits in a quiet residential street in Whitstable, about five minutes' drive from the town centre and seafront. Whitstable is one of those places that people fall for quickly — a small coastal town that manages to be lively without being loud, pretty without being precious, and genuinely welcoming in a way that bigger resorts often aren't. It's also well-placed for exploring the wider Kent coast and some of the county's most visited towns.

Here's what's nearby and what's worth knowing — with accessibility notes where we have them.

---

**[H2 — Block 1]**

### Whitstable

The town itself is the main attraction. The high street runs parallel to the coast and is packed with independent shops, galleries, and places to eat — from the pink-fronted Wheeler's Oyster Bar (a Whitstable institution) to casual seafood shacks on the harbour and proper fish and chip shops along the front. The harbour is a working harbour, which gives the place a texture that purpose-built seaside resorts can't replicate.

Whitstable's beach is shingle, stretching east through Tankerton where a row of colourful beach huts lines the slopes above the shore. The promenade along Tankerton is largely flat and paved, making it one of the more accessible coastal walks in Kent — though it's worth noting that getting onto the beach itself from the prom usually involves a slope onto pebbles, so it depends on your mobility and equipment. The Tankerton slopes are a local favourite for sitting with a takeaway coffee and watching the sea.

The town has a strong food scene. Whitstable is best known for its native oysters (available year-round), but you'll find everything from wood-fired pizza to Vietnamese street food. Many restaurants are in older buildings with varying levels of access, so it's worth checking ahead if step-free entry matters — most are happy to accommodate if you call first.

Local pubs worth mentioning include The Plough on the Canterbury Road, which is close to the property and a stop on the 400 bus route into town and Canterbury. The Oyster Stores on the high street is popular, and The Old Neptune sits directly on the beach.

---

**[H2 — Block 2]**

### Nearby towns worth visiting

**Canterbury** is eight miles south. The cathedral city is a major draw — Canterbury Cathedral, the Beaney House of Art and Knowledge, the cobbled streets of the city centre, and a wide range of restaurants and shops. Canterbury has better wheelchair access infrastructure than many historic cities, with dropped kerbs on most routes and accessible entrances to the cathedral and major attractions. The high street is pedestrianised and mostly flat. Direct buses run from Whitstable to Canterbury regularly (the 400 route stops near the property at The Plough).

**Faversham** is seven miles west along the coast road. It's a market town with a medieval centre, a growing food and drink scene (Shepherd Neame, Britain's oldest brewer, is based here), and a quieter pace than Canterbury. The town centre is fairly compact and mostly flat. Faversham Creek is a pleasant walk from the centre.

**Herne Bay** is four miles east along the coast — another seaside town with a long promenade, a pier (the original was the second longest in the country), and a more traditional bucket-and-spade character. The seafront is largely flat and accessible.

**Margate** is about 16 miles east and home to the Turner Contemporary gallery, the restored Dreamland amusement park, and a regenerated old town. Turner Contemporary has strong accessibility provision, including wheelchair access throughout.

---

**[H2 — Block 3]**

### The Kent countryside

For guests who want green as well as blue, Whitstable sits at the edge of the Kent Downs Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. Blean Woods, a National Nature Reserve, is just a few miles south — though the terrain is uneven and largely unsuitable for wheelchair users. The orchards and farmland between Whitstable and Canterbury are lovely to drive through, and there are several accessible garden attractions in the wider area including Mount Ephraim Gardens (about 30 minutes south) and the Druidstone Park wildlife centre near Blean.

**[H2 — Block 4]**

### Getting here

**By car:** From the M2, take the Thanet Way (A299) exit, then follow signs towards Whitstable via the B2205. The property is at 101 Russell Drive, CT5 2RQ — in a residential area with off-street parking suitable for adapted vehicles. Journey time from central London is around 1.5–2 hours depending on traffic. From Maidstone, allow about 45 minutes via the M2.

**By train:** Whitstable station has direct services to London St Pancras International and London Victoria. The journey takes around 1 hour 15 minutes from St Pancras (high-speed) or around 1 hour 30 minutes from Victoria. Chestfield & Swalecliffe station is a smaller, quieter alternative nearby. From the station, a taxi to the property takes about five minutes.

**By bus:** The Stagecoach 400 route connects Whitstable and Canterbury, with a stop near the property at The Plough pub on Canterbury Road. This route also takes you to the beach and harbour area.

**Accessible taxis:** We recommend Abacus Cars (01227 277745) for wheelchair-accessible vehicles. Pre-booking is advisable, especially during school-run times when accessible vehicles are in higher demand.

---

**[H2 — Block 5]**

### Getting around during your stay

Whitstable is a small town and many things are within a short drive. The seafront and town centre are about five to ten minutes by car or 20–30 minutes on foot from the property. The 400 bus runs regularly if you don't want to drive.

For wider exploration — Canterbury, Faversham, Herne Bay — you'll find a car is the most practical option, though rail links exist to all three. Canterbury is the easiest to reach by bus.

If you need to stock up, the nearest supermarkets are Sainsbury's in Chestfield (4-minute drive), Tesco Extra on Millstrood Road (7 minutes), and Aldi at Prospect Retail Park (10 minutes).
