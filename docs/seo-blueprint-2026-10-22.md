### R4. October 2026 facts that change the copy

1. **FAQ rich results are gone.** Google stopped showing them for every site on **7 May 2026** and removed the documentation on 15 June. FAQPage markup is still valid, and Google says it still uses it to understand pages, but it no longer earns a bigger snippet.
2. **Google's own AI guide (May 2026)** says AI Overviews and AI Mode use normal ranking. It says no special markup or llms.txt is needed, and there's "no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces". It calls first-hand, original content the thing to have. So the brief's "45-word answer" is good for readers and for People Also Ask, not an AI trick.
3. **VisitEngland's National Accessible Scheme ended on 31 July 2025.** VisitEngland now calls access statements "**Accessibility Guides**". As of 7 April 2026, its recommended detailed format is an **AccessAble Detailed Access Guide**. The brief's "NAS terminology" is out of date.
4. **Direct payments.** The statutory guidance (updated 22 July 2025) has no holiday-specific rule. Councils decide. Google's AI answer leads with "**not for holiday accommodation, yes for the care elements in your support plan**", so our post has to start there.
5. **Revitalise** closed its centres in November 2024. It now gives grants, typically about £500 a person, to disabled adults and family carers.
6. **Changing Places:** there's one at **Whitstable Harbour** (opened August 2015, listed by Canterbury City Council), plus Herne Bay and Reculver.
7. **Beach Within Reach** lends beach wheelchairs at **Thanet** beaches (Minnis Bay, St Mildred's, Westbrook, Stone Bay, Viking Bay), not in Whitstable. Users must be able to transfer without a hoist.

| `noindex` Privacy and Terms | They compete with nothing. | Both stay indexed for brand searches. |
| Question H2s everywhere | You asked on 17 Oct for headings that name what's there. | Question H2s where a section answers something; naming H2s for rooms and kit. |
| "No two URLs share an intent" | 21 URLs, four intents. | Read as: no two URLs share a **query and intent pair**. |

**One house, one audience (23 Oct).** Don't sort guests into groups. Name people together ("wheelchair users, people with learning disabilities, families, friends, and people who come with a PA or on their own"), then describe what the house and the care offer everyone. Sort by need (the kit, the care, the paperwork), never by diagnosis. No page, section, tab or card exists for one condition, and no keyword gets a page of its own for one group.

**Rules carried over:** never "fully accessible" outside guest quotes; never "patients"; no em-dashes; care never implied free, included or arranged "separately"; no care lead times; Keelie and Nikki by name only; guest quotes word for word. **Banned words:** nestled, boasts, beacon, tapestry, symphony, embark, journey, sanctuary, oasis, bespoke, unparalleled, seamless, holistic, empower, burden, exhausted carer, tirelessly, loved one's needs, unlock, peace of mind, look no further, elevate.

## 3. The Flesch 72 cheat-sheet

Flesch = 206.835 − 1.015 × (words per sentence) − 84.6 × (syllables per word). Syllables matter about 80 times more than sentence length, so the lever is word choice, not chopping sentences.

| Average words per sentence | Most syllables per word that still scores 72 |
| ---: | ---: |
| 11 | 1.46 |
| 13 | 1.44 |
| 15 | 1.41 |
| 20 | 1.35 |

**Pay it back:** a sentence carrying a five- or six-syllable word is followed by one made of short words. The example sentences below pool to **Flesch 72.8 at 14.7 words per sentence**, with every hard word kept.

| Word | Use instead, where you can | When the word has to stay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| accessibility (6) | "access" | Our accessibility page lists every door width, so you can check your chair before you book. |
| accessible (4) | Keep it as a keyword; elsewhere "step-free", "level" | It's an accessible bungalow on one floor, and the hoist is ready when you arrive. |
| accommodation (5) | "the stay", "the house" | Some councils will pay for the accommodation too, but that's their call, so ask early. |
| wheelchair accessible (6) | "step-free", plus the millimetres | The front door is wheelchair accessible, with a 965mm gap and no step. |
| domiciliary care (7) | "home care" | Domiciliary care just means care at home, so the carers come to you here. |
| assessment (3) | "a chat about what you need" | Your council may want to do a needs assessment first, so ask how long it takes. |
| occupational therapist (8) | "OT" after first use | If your occupational therapist needs the bed height, it's on the access page. |
| equipment (3) | "kit", or name it | The equipment is in the price, but please bring your own sling so it fits. |
| profiling bed (4) | Explain it once | A profiling bed lifts your head and knees at the press of a button. |
